# Master Guide: Classroom Apps for Non-Coders

This master guide compiles the documentation for all five custom classroom applications. Every app runs directly in any modern web browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari, Firefox), requires zero software installations, uses no outside downloads, and protects student data privacy 100% offline.

---

# Example 1: EduSpin Wheel Picker

## What is this app?
This is a fun, colorful digital spinning wheel that runs right inside your internet browser. You can use it as a single wheel to pick one thing at random, or put two wheels side-by-side to pair things up or compare ideas.

## How to use it in a virtual K-12 classroom
* **Random Student Caller:** Paste your student roster into the wheel. When it is time to answer questions or read aloud, give it a spin. The exciting tick sounds and visual spin keep every student watching their screen.
* **Fair Turns:** Turn on the setting to remove names after they are picked so nobody gets picked twice in a row.
* **Side-by-Side Compare & Contrast:** Put historical figures on Wheel 1 and scientific events on Wheel 2, or characters on one and story conflicts on the other. Spin both and have students explain how the two items connect.
* **100% Student Privacy:** You do not need to create an account, log in, or upload student names to the cloud. Your class roster stays safely on your own computer.

## What a non-coder learns from prompting this app
* **Your browser is a free player:** You do not need expensive software subscriptions to run classroom tools. A simple file ending in `.html` can open right in Chrome or Edge and work instantly.
* **No missing parts:** When asking AI for sound or pictures, you can tell it to create sounds using the computer's built-in sound card and draw shapes with simple math. That way, you never have to download extra sound or image files that might get lost or blocked by school firewalls.
* **You can change how it works:** If you want two wheels instead of one, you simply ask the AI in plain English: "Add an option for two wheels side-by-side."

# Example 2: FeedbackFlow (Grading Feedback Builder)

## What is this app?
This is a point-and-click grading assistant. Instead of typing the same comments over and over on 80 student papers, you click buttons for the grade levels and pre-set comments you want. The app instantly turns your clicks into a warm, complete paragraph of feedback that you can copy and paste with one touch.

## How to use it in a virtual K-12 classroom
* **Speed Up Grading Without Sounding Like a Robot:** Keep this open on half of your screen while viewing student work on the other half. Click through criteria like "Main Idea", "Evidence", or "Grammar", and watch a smooth paragraph form right before your eyes.
* **Quick Common Notes:** Add quick one-click buttons for phrases you use all the time, such as "Please check office hours for help" or "Remember to cite two sources."
* **Grade Summary Included:** The updated version can automatically calculate scores (like 10/12) and attach a tidy breakdown at the end of the note.
* **Save Your Rubrics:** You can save your rubric to a small backup file on your computer and open it again whenever that assignment comes around next term.

## What a non-coder learns from prompting this app
* **Apps can evolve in stages:** You do not have to imagine the perfect app on your first try. Version 1 started with a simple 0 to 4 rating system. Version 2 was created simply by asking: *"Can I also change the score numbers, add custom levels, and show a total score at the bottom?"*
* **Ask for what you want in plain words:** You do not need to know programming words like "variables" or "arrays." You just tell the AI: "When I click level 3, write this sentence, and give me a button that copies everything to my clipboard."
* **Complete student privacy:** Because this app runs only on your screen, no student grades or personal data ever travel over the internet.

# Example 3: Elastic Timeline

## What is this app?
This is an interactive timeline game where cards float gently at the bottom of the screen. You or your students can drag and drop the cards onto a timeline bar at the top, and the cards smoothly slide apart to make room. When you click "Check Timeline," the app lights up the cards to show which ones are in the right order.

## How to use it in a virtual K-12 classroom
* **Chronology Review During Screen Shares:** Share your screen during a live virtual lesson. Read the floating events aloud with the class, ask students in the chat or microphone what comes next, and drag the cards into place together.
* **Smart Color Feedback:** When you click "Check Timeline," cards placed in the correct chronological story turn bright green. Cards placed out of order glow yellow so students can see where the chain broke without feeling defeated.
* **Instant Custom Lessons:** Click the "Edit List" button and paste any list of events in order (events from the American Revolution, plot points from a novel, or the stages of the water cycle). The app shuffles them and builds a brand-new game instantly.
* **Celebration Confetti:** When the whole sequence is solved correctly, colorful confetti bursts across the screen to celebrate student success!

## What a non-coder learns from prompting this app
* **You can describe physical movement:** You can tell the AI how you want things to look and feel using normal words like *"make unplaced cards float gently like they are in water"* or *"slide cards apart to make room when dragging."*
* **Tell the AI to keep everything in one file:** If you do not tell the AI to keep everything together, it might try to connect to outside web tools that school filters block. Telling the AI *"keep everything in a single standalone file with no outside links"* ensures it works 100% offline on any school computer.
* **Borrow fun ideas from popular games:** Asking the AI to use familiar rules—like Wordle-style green and yellow feedback—helps create engaging educational games in seconds.

# Example 4: Vocabulary Codenames

## What is this app?
This is a classroom party-game board for reviewing vocabulary, based on the popular game Codenames. You paste in any list of 16 or more vocabulary words, and the app lays them out as cards in a 4-by-4 grid with secret team assignments hidden underneath.

## How to use it in a virtual K-12 classroom
* **Engaging Team Review:** Divide your class into a Red Team and a Blue Team during a live class or in breakout rooms.
* **Instant Secret Keys for Student Leaders:** Each team chooses a "Spymaster" (clue giver). With one click, the teacher can copy the secret red or blue word lists and direct-message them in Zoom, Microsoft Teams, or Google Meet. The clue-givers give one-word clues to help their team guess the right cards on your shared screen.
* **Interactive Card Reveals:** As students guess words on your screen, click each card to flip it over and show its real identity: Red, Blue, Neutral, or the game-ending "Black Card" (the surprise trap card).
* **Peek Mode for Teachers:** Turn on the teacher peek toggle to see subtle colored borders on every card so you know the full board without spoiling the surprise for the class.

## What a non-coder learns from prompting this app
* **AI can solve your virtual teaching hurdles:** Notice how this app includes one-click buttons to copy secret lists ready to paste into chat windows. You can tell the AI about your everyday virtual classroom annoyances, and it will build exact shortcuts for them.
* **No images needed to make games:** You do not need to hunt for graphics or clip art. The AI can build sharp cards, glowing borders, and icons using standard browser colors and text symbols.
* **Safe and zero-prep:** No accounts, student passwords, or logins required. Paste your spelling or science words, hit generate, and play immediately.

# Example 5: Rotate & Relate (Geometry Triangle Sandbox)

## What is this app?
This is a hands-on geometry manipulative for teaching similar right triangles. In math class, when an altitude line cuts through a big right triangle, it creates smaller triangles that are turned upside down and backward. This app lets students connect matching sides using virtual colored cords, type out math similarity statements, and press a button to watch the triangles magically lift off the page, flip over, and rotate until they line up side-by-side.

## How to use it in a virtual K-12 classroom
* **Make Invisible Math Visible:** Instead of struggling to see how overlapping shapes connect on a flat textbook page, students see live shapes on your shared screen.
* **Hands-on Virtual Wires:** Students or the teacher can drag red, blue, and yellow wires between corresponding short sides, long sides, and diagonal sides to test their thinking.
* **The "Aha!" Moment Animation:** When students click to check their answer, the app animates the transformation—gliding, flipping, and rotating the triangles together so students instantly see whether their colored lines match up or clash.
* **Unlimited Fresh Practice:** Click "New Triangle" to get a brand-new randomized shape with different angles and letters for endless practice problems.

## What a non-coder learns from prompting this app
* **Clear plans lead to amazing apps:** When asking AI for something complex, giving it a clear blueprint (like explaining step-by-step: "Draw the shape, give me 3 colored wires, and animate the rotation") produces results that look like commercial software.
* **How web pages are built (The 3 Pieces):** This example shows how web pages work behind the scenes:
  1. *The Skeleton* (`Elements_On_Screen.html`): The words, buttons, and shapes you see.
  2. *The Clothes* (`How_It_Looks.css`): The colors, fonts, and dark theme.
  3. *The Brain* (`What_It_Does.js`): The math, mouse dragging, and smooth animations.
* **You are in full control:** You do not need to settle for clunky, locked-down textbook websites. With a simple, clear description, AI can craft an interactive manipulative tailored to the exact topic your students are struggling with.
